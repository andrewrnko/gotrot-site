Dry Rot Repair in Woodinville, the short version.

Dry rot is a fungus eating wet wood. The name is wrong. It only looks dry once it's done. In Seattle and the rest of Western Washington, wood stays wet for months at a time, so rot shows up in the same places on most houses: window trim, the bottom row of siding, deck ledgers, roof edges and sill plates.

The soft spot you can see is usually the small part. Water travels along flashing, sheathing and framing before it shows. So we start at the spot you found and work outward until the wood pushes back. Sometimes that's one board. Sometimes it's the corner of a wall. You see what we find before we fix it.

Then we fix what let the water in. Replace the wood and leave the leak, and the rot comes back in the same spot. That's the whole job, every time, whether the house is in Bellevue, Everett or Lynnwood.

dry rot repair questions

The questionswe always get.

  • Can dry rot spread?

    It spreads wherever the wood stays wet. Above about 20 percent moisture, the fungus can grow into the next board. Stop the water and dry the wood, and it stops.

  • Can rotted wood be repaired, or does it have to be replaced?

    Small, dry spots that don't hold anything up can sometimes be filled. Anything structural gets cut out and replaced. We tell you which is which after we probe it.

  • What affects the cost of dry rot repair?

    How far the rot goes, whether framing is involved, access, finish materials, and what it takes to fix the water. A photo can't show all of that, so we look first. Estimates are free for eligible projects.

  • How long does a dry rot repair take?

    Most repairs take 1 to 3 days. Larger structural jobs take a week or more. You get the schedule with your estimate.

  • Do you fix the leak too?

    Yes. Finding and fixing the water is part of the repair. If the source is the roof covering or plumbing, we tell you, because that's another trade.
