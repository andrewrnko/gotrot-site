Where's the water getting in?

tap a letter.

Rot is the symptom. Water is the cause. We don't close a wall until we know which one it came through.

B. Where the gutter meets the wall. Water comes off the roof edge and runs down the siding right there, every storm.

Both photos in our quiz have the same problem in slightly different spots. Water coming off a roof has to go somewhere. If nothing turns it away from the wall at the bottom of the roof edge, the wall gets it. That one detail rots more siding than almost anything else we see.

why the order matters

Fix the wood without the water and the new wood rots in the same spot. Fix the water without the wood and you've stopped the cause but left a soft board holding something up. The repair is both, in that order.

It also changes how big the job is. When we find the water first, we know which way the rot probably traveled, and we open the wall in the right direction. When someone starts with the wood, they tend to stop at the first board that looks fine, and the rest gets closed back up.

  1. 01

    find the water

    Start at the rot and look uphill: flashing, gutter, joint, grading.

  2. 02

    stop it

    Kickout, head flashing, a downspout extension, clearance from the soil.

  3. 03

    cut it out

    Remove the rotted wood back to solid wood on every side.

  4. 04

    rebuild

    New framing, sheathing, siding or trim, primed on all six sides.

  5. 05

    check it

    Look at the same spot after the next big storm.

two code numbers worth knowing

Kickout flashing where a roof eave meets a sidewall (IRC R903.2.1). Wood siding and trim 6 inches above soil and 2 above concrete (IRC R317.1).

Walk around your house once this fall with those spots in mind and a screwdriver in your hand. If something sinks in, we work in Seattle, on the Eastside and across Snohomish County.

Look up from anything soft before you look anywhere else. Rot is almost always below where the water gets in, so the cause is usually a few feet above the damage, not next to it.
