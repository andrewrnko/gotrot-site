Fix it nowor in spring?

It's October and the rain is back. Fix the rot now or wait for spring? Now, if you can. Here's why, and what to do if it has to wait.

It's October. The rain is back. Is it worth fixing rot now, or can it wait until spring? Short answer: now, if you can.

now vs spring

fix it nowwait for spring
the woodstops getting wettersoaks all winter
the rotstays about the size it ishas months to spread
the jobusually smallerusually bigger
the workhappens around the weatherdry and easy

why waiting usually costs more

Rot fungi grow when wood stays above about 20 percent moisture. Western Washington's wet season runs from fall well into spring, so a leak that's open in October can keep wood wet for months straight. That's months for the rot to move into the next board, the sheathing behind it, or the joist under it.

Rot also tends to grow in steps. A soft trim board lets water into the sheathing behind it. Soft sheathing lets it into the stud. Each step makes the next one faster, and each one makes the repair bigger. Catching it at the trim board is the cheap version.

now or spring?

You find blistered paint on a deck stair in October. Fix it now or in spring?

what if it has to wait?

Sometimes the schedule or the budget says spring. That's fine. Just don't let the wood sit open to the weather all winter. A few things buy you time.

can you work in the rain?

A lot of rot repair can happen year-round. Some finish work, like paint, waits for a dry day. We plan around it.

get it looked at either way

If you're on the fence, get it looked at now even if the work waits. Knowing how far the rot goes makes the decision easy. A soft board with a fixed leak can wait. A soft ledger can't. Neither can a stair someone uses every day.

We do rot repair through the winter in Seattle, on the Eastside and in Snohomish County. If the work needs to wait for dry weather, we'll tell you that too.
